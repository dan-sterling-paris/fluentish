Entry Assessment

1

The Greeting

Open with "Bonjour ! Comment allez-vous ?" at natural pace.

Zero signal: Complete freeze, nervous laugh, "I have no idea what you said"
Rustbuster signal: Produces something, even "Bien, merci" however haltingly
2

The Word Harvest

"Say any French words you know. Anything at all. Food, places, random things. Just fire them out."

0-5 words
True zero
6-20 words
Passive rustbuster
20+ words
Active rustbuster
3

The Accent Check

Say three words and ask them to repeat: bonjour, merci, s'il vous plait.

Listen for: nasal vowels (the "on" in bonjour), French r vs English r, final-syllable stress.

4

The Goal Anchor

"When you imagine yourself speaking French successfully, what does that scene look like?"

Holiday survival Relocation readiness Professional French Personal enrichment
5

The Sentence Attempt

"Try to tell me one thing about yourself in French. Anything. Even one word is fine."

Zero: "Je suis [name]" or nothing
Rustbuster: "J'habite a Manchester" or similar

Progressive Overload

Five measurable dimensions. Push one harder each week. At The Landing, tell the student which one improved.

At The Landing: "Last week your longest sentence was 6 words. Today you hit 14. That's the progress." Always name one specific overload dimension that improved. Make the weight increase visible.

Comprehension Principle

Comprehension training uses only material the student has already learned to produce. This means listening exercises reinforce production rather than creating a separate cognitive burden.

The exception: authentic audio clips where the student's task is to identify known frames amid noise (not understand everything). This teaches the real-world skill of extracting meaning from partial comprehension.
