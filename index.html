Sound familiar?

The problem isn't your French.
It's how you learnt it.

01

The App Problem

Language apps are excellent at making you feel like you're learning French. They are considerably less excellent at preparing you for a conversation with an actual French person. Games teach you to play games. They do not teach you to listen, respond, and hold your nerve.

02

The Holiday Panic Problem

You know what you want. You know roughly how to ask for it. What you have not prepared for is what happens when they answer back, at full speed, with regional vowels, while someone behind you sighs. You nod. You have no idea what they said. You order the wrong thing. Again.

03

The Textbook Problem

GCSE French taught you to describe your bedroom, your family, and a fictional holiday in Perpignan. It did not teach you to argue a restaurant bill, negotiate with a landlord, or hold your own at a dinner party when the conversation takes a turn you weren't expecting. Real life is not in textbooks. It never was.

The FluentISH philosophy

Not fluent.
Not basic.
FluentISH.

The name is intentional. The goal isn't academic perfection. It isn't to conjugate the subjunctive in your sleep or recite Moliere at parties (though good on you if that's the dream).

The goal is to get you to FluentISH: the point where you can hold a conversation without your internal monologue screaming, where you understand the reply, and where you come across as a person rather than a confused tourist holding a phrasebook upside down.

That is absolutely achievable. It requires good conversation, good coffee, and someone who actually knows what they're talking about.
