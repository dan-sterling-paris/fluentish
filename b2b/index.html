The French connection problem.

01

The Google Translate Problem

Machine translation gets the words across. It does not get the register, the tone, or the cultural subtext. French business relationships run on nuance. An email that reads as blunt, informal, or just slightly off will cost you more than you think.

02

The Bilingual Intern Problem

A colleague who "did French at school" is not a bilingual professional. They'll manage pleasantries. They will not handle a contract negotiation, a client dispute, or a supplier relationship that needs careful managing. And they have their actual job to do.

03

The Full-Time Hire Problem

A bilingual Business Development Manager earns £40–55k. You don't need forty hours a week of French — you need four, done properly, when it matters. A full-time hire is the wrong solution to a part-time problem.

The solution

A fractional French lead
changes all of that.

I become your French-speaking professional, part-time. Not a translator. Not a tutor. Your embedded French lead — on call when you need it, paid only for what you use.

Client calls & meetings

I join your French calls as your bilingual lead. Introductions, negotiations, relationship maintenance — handled properly, in French, to the standard your clients expect.

Email & written comms

French emails drafted or reviewed before they're sent. Proposals checked for tone and register. Correspondence that sounds like it was written by someone who actually speaks French — because it was.

Document & contract review

French documents checked for comprehension before you sign or respond. Not legal advice — but knowing what something actually says, and what the implications are, is not nothing.

Business culture advisory

French business culture is different. Relationship norms, meeting etiquette, what to say and what absolutely not to — I've worked in it for fifteen years. That knowledge prevents expensive mistakes.
