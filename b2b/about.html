Background

Not a translator. Not a tutor. A bilingual professional.

Most people who offer French language support to businesses come from one of two places: they're qualified translators, or they're language teachers. I come from neither.

I moved to Paris in 2011. I've worked in France — in meetings, in negotiations, in client relationships, in finance and in entertainment — as a fully bilingual professional for over a decade. Not as someone who speaks French at work. As someone for whom French is one of two working languages, with no hierarchy between them.

That's a different thing. And it's the thing that makes a fractional French lead useful to a business rather than just a bilingual freelancer they've hired for a project.

Why this matters for your business

Three things that set this apart.

01

Disney standard, client-facing French

For eight years, Disney cast me as the English and French voice of a well-known animated character. I am the only person ever given that role in both languages.

The show was live. The French audience was real. There is no more demanding proof of bilingual fluency than performing live in both languages, professionally, to an audience that will notice the difference.

What this means for you: client-facing French that represents your business well

02

10+ years working bilingually in business

Finance and entertainment. Both sectors. Both languages. Contracts, negotiations, client relationships, internal communications — I've done all of it in French, to the professional standard those sectors demand.

I understand what French business communication is trying to achieve, not just what the words mean. That's the difference between translation and genuine bilingual professional support.

What this means for you: someone who understands business context, not just language

03

Fifteen years embedded in France

Paris since 2011. Also Bordeaux. I maintain an apartment in Paris. I spend significant time in France every year. This is not an academic relationship with French culture — it's an active, current one.

French business norms change. Relationship expectations shift. What works in 2026 is not what worked in 2010. Being actively embedded means the advice and support I give reflects how France actually operates now.

What this means for you: current, lived cultural knowledge, not theory

Why fractional

You don't need forty hours a week of French.

A full-time bilingual hire costs £40–55k before employer NI and benefits. That's the right answer for a business running French operations daily. It's the wrong answer for most Manchester SMEs.

What most businesses with French connections actually need is four to eight hours a month, done properly — not an underskilled workaround, and not the cost of a full-time headcount.

The fractional model exists because the need is real but part-time. You get the bilingual professional. You don't pay for the hours you don't use.
