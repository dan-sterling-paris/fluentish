What's included

French support,
done properly.

The retainer covers four areas of French-language business support. Here's exactly what each one means in practice.

01

Client calls & meetings

I join your French calls and meetings as your bilingual lead. Whether that's a first introduction with a new French supplier, a progress call with a Paris client, or a delicate conversation that needs handling carefully — I'm there as your French-speaking professional.

48 hours' notice preferred for scheduled calls. For urgent situations, we'll talk about what's workable on the discovery call.

Covered by the retainer

  • + New supplier or client introductions
  • + Ongoing relationship calls and check-ins
  • + Negotiation support and sensitive conversations
  • + Pre-call briefing and post-call debrief
  • + Video or phone — whichever the situation requires

02

Email & written communications

French business email has its own register, its own conventions, and its own way of beginning and ending correspondence that baffles almost every non-native speaker. I draft French emails, review what you've written, and make sure every message sounds professional — not like it came from Google Translate.

Same-day turnaround on standard requests. We'll agree a response window that works for your business on the discovery call.

Covered by the retainer

  • + Drafting French emails and correspondence
  • + Reviewing and correcting what you've already written
  • + Proposals, quotes, and formal correspondence
  • + Tone and register guidance (how formal to be, and when)
  • + Inbound French emails translated and summarised

03

Document & contract review

French contracts, supplier agreements, and official documents can sit in your inbox for weeks because nobody wants to make a decision without understanding what they actually say. I read them, summarise the key points, flag anything that needs attention, and tell you what questions to be asking your solicitor.

This is not legal advice. It is comprehension and context — the difference between knowing what a document says and having to trust that it's fine.

Covered by the retainer

  • + Supplier agreements and commercial contracts
  • + Terms and conditions, invoices, official notices
  • + Plain-English summaries of key clauses
  • + Flagging unusual terms or points needing legal review

04

Business culture advisory

French business culture has rules. Some are written down. Most aren't. How you open a meeting, how directly you express disagreement, how you build a supplier relationship over time — these things vary significantly from British business norms, and getting them wrong has consequences.

Fifteen years of living and working bilingually in France means I know where the traps are. This is knowledge that prevents expensive, embarrassing, and entirely avoidable mistakes.

Covered by the retainer

  • + Pre-meeting briefings on French business etiquette
  • + Relationship-building advice for French counterparts
  • + Situational guidance — what to say, what to avoid
  • + Market context for France, Belgium, or French Canada

How it works in practice

The async model.

The retainer is not a set number of weekly hours. It's an embedded relationship. Here's how that actually works.

1

Monthly scope call

30 minutes · Start of month

We begin each month with a quick call to align on what's coming up — upcoming meetings, pending correspondence, anything needing French support in the next four weeks.

2

Async requests

Email or message · Same-day

Send requests as they come up. An email to review, a document to summarise, a question about how to handle something. Same-day turnaround on standard requests.

3

Live calls when needed

48 hrs notice · Via Zoom or phone

For calls and meetings requiring live French support, 48 hours' notice is standard. I'll join as your bilingual lead, and we'll debrief afterwards.
