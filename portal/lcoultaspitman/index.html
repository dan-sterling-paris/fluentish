Get-Out-of-Jail Cards

Keep these loaded. When you're stuck mid-conversation, reach for one instead of freezing up.

Comment dire…
ko-mon deer
How can I say this… (buys you time)
Pardon ?
par-don
Sorry? / I didn't catch that
C'est-à-dire…
set-ah-deer
That is to say… (regroup mid-sentence)
Ça veut dire…
sa vuh deer
That means… (clarify what you just said)
Key Concepts

The Past Tense Shortcut

Add an "é" sound to make most verbs past tense. Don't overthink conjugation.

Je pense → J'ai pensé zhuh ponss → zhay pon-say I think → I thought
Je mange → J'ai mangé zhuh monzh → zhay mon-zhay I eat → I ate

Making "Il y a" Singular

Replace "des" with "un" (masculine), "une" (feminine), or "du/de la" (uncountable).

Il y a des fleurs → Il y a une fleur
ee-lee-ah day flur → ee-lee-ah oon flur
There are flowers → There is a flower
Il y a de la neige
ee-lee-ah duh la nezh
There is some snow

The Canada Story

You say "en France" (feminine country) but "au Canada" (masculine country). There's no intuitive rule. The French prioritise how things sound over strict logic. Keep this anecdote locked and loaded for when anyone criticises your French.

Frames & Fillers

What You Covered

Three foundational frames: il y a des ___ (there are some), je pense que c'est ___ (I think it's), and il y a [modifier] de ___ (there are lots/too many/never any of). You proved that the cognitive load sits on the English, not the French, by rattling off 20 nouns in a minute without hesitating on the frame.

You then learned 8 modifiers that slot into the third frame, giving you 168 sentence combinations.

You also explored the past tense shortcut (add an "é" sound), singular forms of "il y a", and the bonus frame "je me suis dit" which sounds far more natural than the textbook "j'ai pensé".
