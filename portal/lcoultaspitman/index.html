Florist, flowers & frames

Your 3 Frames: The Building Blocks

These three frames are your foundation. Slot any filler word onto the end and you've got a sentence. Don't overthink it, just play.

Frame 1: Il y a des…

Il y a des…
ee-lee-ah day
There are some…
Il y a des difficultés
ee-lee-ah day dee-fee-kool-tay
There are some difficulties
Il y a des fleurs
ee-lee-ah day flur
There are some flowers
Il y a des saucisses
ee-lee-ah day so-sees
There are some sausages

Also works singular: "il y a un…" (masc) / "il y a une…" (fem) / "il y a du…" (uncountable, e.g. snow)

Frame 2: Je pense que c'est…

Je pense que c'est…
zhuh ponss kuh say
I think it's…
Je pense que c'est difficile
zhuh ponss kuh say dee-fee-seel
I think it's difficult
Je pense que c'est fun
zhuh ponss kuh say fun
I think it's fun
Je pense que c'est difficile parce qu'il y a beaucoup de problèmes
zhuh ponss kuh say dee-fee-seel parss kee-lee-ah bo-koo duh pro-blem
I think it's difficult because there are a lot of problems

Frame 3: Il y a [modifier] de…

Il y a beaucoup de…
ee-lee-ah bo-koo duh
There are a lot of…
Il y a trop de…
ee-lee-ah tro duh
There are too many…
Il y a plein de…
ee-lee-ah plan duh
There are loads of…
Il y a jamais de…
ee-lee-ah zhah-may duh
There are never any…

Swap in any of the 7 modifiers (see below) to change the meaning. With 7 modifiers and 21 filler words, that's 147 sentences from this one frame.

Bonus Frame: Je me suis dit

This sounds way more natural than "j'ai pensé" which is textbook but clunky. Think of it like saying "I was thinking…" in English rather than "I have thought…"

Je me suis dit…
zhuh muh swee dee
I was thinking… / I said to myself…
Je me suis dit qu'on pourrait aller au parc
zhuh muh swee dee kon poo-ray ah-lay oh park
I was thinking we could go to the park
Je me suis dit qu'on pourrait aller au pub
zhuh muh swee dee kon poo-ray ah-lay oh pub
I was thinking we could go to the pub
Je me suis dit qu'on pourrait dîner
zhuh muh swee dee kon poo-ray dee-nay
I was thinking we could have dinner
Get-Out-of-Jail Cards

Keep these loaded at all times. When you're stuck mid-conversation, reach for one instead of freezing up.

Comment dire…
ko-mon deer
How can I say this… (buys you time)
Pardon ?
par-don
Sorry? / I didn't catch that
C'est-à-dire…
set-ah-deer
That is to say… (regroup mid-sentence)
Ça veut dire…
sa vuh deer
That means… (clarify what you just said)

Key Concepts

The Past Tense Shortcut

To make most verbs past tense, just add an "é" sound to the end. Don't overthink conjugation, let your ear do the work over time.

Je pense → J'ai pensé zhuh ponss → zhay pon-say I think → I thought
Je mange → J'ai mangé zhuh monzh → zhay mon-zhay I eat → I ate

Making "Il y a" Singular

Switch from plural to singular by replacing "des" with "un" (masculine) or "une" (feminine), or "du" for uncountable things.

Il y a des fleurs → Il y a une fleur
ee-lee-ah day flur → ee-lee-ah oon flur
There are flowers → There is a flower
Il y a de la neige
ee-lee-ah duh la nezh
There is some snow

The Canada Story

You say "en France" (feminine country) but "au Canada" (masculine country). There's no intuitive rule for why. The French prioritise how things sound over strict logic. Keep this anecdote locked and loaded for when anyone criticises your French.

Sounding Natural
Tête dans les nuages tet don lay noo-azh Head in the clouds
En plein hiver on plan ee-vair In the middle of winter
En plein été on plan ay-tay In the middle of summer
Pas mal de pah mal duh Not a bad amount of / quite a lot of
