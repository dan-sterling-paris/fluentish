The Buying Time Toolkit - when you don't know a word
Comment on dit ? com-on on dee How does one say…? Ask for any word in French - keeps you in the language
C'est quoi en français ? say kwah on fron-say What's that in French?
C'est quoi en anglais ? say kwah on on-glay What's that in English? (last resort - if you draw a complete blank)
Pour dire… poor deer To say… (reaching for a word mid-sentence)
Donc… donk So… (filler - buys thinking time. Dan uses it without realising.)
[the puff] pfff A legitimate French thinking noise. Use it freely.

"Comment on dit ?" sounds natural - as if you're reaching for the right word, not giving up. It keeps the conversation in French.

The BANGS Rule

Adjectives go after the noun in French - except these five categories, which always go before.

B Beauty beau, joli
A Age jeune, vieux
N Number premier, deux
G Goodness bon, mauvais
S Size grand, petit
un grand vina great wine
un petit peua little bit
la première foisthe first time
un bon fromagea good cheese
un bel hommea handsome man (beau → bel before vowel)
Cultural anecdote

The Second World War is La Seconde Guerre mondiale - not deuxième. "Seconde" implies it is the last in a series. Ask a French person why - they'll love it.

The Plus Trap

Spelled identically. The S changes everything.

Plus (say the S)
MORE
"Je voudrais plus de fromage."
I'd like more cheese.
Plus (silent S)
NO MORE
"Je ne veux plus de fromage."
I don't want any more.

Think of it as saying a fuller, longer word when you mean more - you'll keep the S naturally.

"Encore !" for another round is more French than "plus" - unambiguous and completely natural.

Sounding Parisian - three things that signal you've been around
Chais pas
shay pa
Dunno. Parisians almost never say the full "Je ne sais pas" in casual conversation. Always with a slight shrug and throw-away delivery. Do this once and they'll know you've been around.
Ah bon ?
a bon
Oh really? Almost always a question - a touch of genuine surprise. A slight lift at the end. "Ah bon !" means agreement; "Ah bon ?" means surprise. Use the question version when something catches you off guard.
Bah non / Bah si
ba non / ba see
"Bah non" = no way / oh come on. "Bah si" = oh but yes! (used to contradict a negative - the French "oh yes it is"). Not a warm or friendly sound - a little confrontational. Save it for when you're mildly outraged or pushing back.
