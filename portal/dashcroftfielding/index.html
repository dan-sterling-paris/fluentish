Sounding Natural

Accent fundamentals

Your Welsh vowels are already doing you favours: that rounded mouth shape is closer to French than flat English is. The main habit to build: relax the R and keep sounds further back in the mouth.

Merci beaucoup
mair-see bo-koo
Thank you very much

Watch the "beaucoup": rush the vowels and it drifts toward two words you really don't want to say to a waiter. Keep it deep and round: bo​-koo, not bo-kew.

The "On" Trick

In textbooks, "we" is nous. In real life, almost nobody says nous in casual conversation. The French say on instead. On va à Paris (we're going to Paris), on mange (we're eating). It also works as a vague "people" or "one": on me considère (people consider me). One word, two jobs, and it makes you sound immediately less like a textbook.

A Little Etymology
Un compagnon
un kom-pan-yon
A companion

Com- (with) + pain (bread): a companion is literally someone you share bread with. A lot of French vocabulary is hiding in plain sight like this once you know where to look for the English cousin underneath the accent.

Also: if you ever see a bottle of "La Vieille Ferme" (the rooster label) in the supermarket, now you know exactly what it means. The Old Farm.

Technique: The Window Pane

Think of stretching pizza dough. You work it a little, it goes slightly transparent. Keep going, a bit more transparent. Keep going until a hole appears. That's where you need more practice.

1

Take one phrase at a time. Say it five times as fast as you can. Don't worry about accuracy, just get through it.

2

Stretch to two phrases. Repeat until smooth. When a hole appears (you trip), stop there and drill that join.

3

Keep stretching until you can run the whole script in one go. Speed first, polish later.

You're a visual learner: you see the shape of the sentence. So record yourself reading the script correctly, then listen and repeat. That links the shape to the sound, and the sound is what will come out when you need it.

Output & Roleplay

Speaking under pressure · Recasting · Professional vocabulary
What You Covered

Big milestone: you did 7 minutes of uninterrupted French in a roleplay exercise. We used the recasting technique (I repeat what you said back to you with the grammar corrected, without breaking the conversation). The focus shifted towards professional terminology and tangible subjects to give you more to say. We also talked about the output hypothesis: speaking is what builds fluency, not just listening. Your brain notices the gap between what you want to say and what you can say, and that's where the learning happens.

Key Concepts

Recasting

When you make a mistake mid-conversation, I repeat your sentence back with the correction slotted in. You don't stop, you don't freeze, you just hear the right version and carry on. This keeps the flow going and lets you absorb corrections naturally.

The Output Hypothesis

The idea that actively producing language (speaking, writing) is what drives acquisition, not just understanding input. When you try to say something and can't quite get there, your brain flags the gap. That "noticing gap" is where real learning happens. Listening is necessary, but speaking is what makes it stick.

Frames & Slots

Core frames · Weaving technique · Paris trip planning
What You Covered

We introduced the frame-and-slot method: high-frequency sentence starters that you can drop any noun or idea into. Three core frames to start: il y a (there is/are), je pense que (I think that), and si (if). We also practised "weaving," switching to English mid-sentence when you don't have the French word, then carrying on in French. Keeps you talking instead of freezing. You hit 99% accuracy in the translation drills. Paris trip booked: 13 hours of tutoring planned, including 3 sessions on-site.

Core Frames

Je pense que…

Si…

Key Concepts

Weaving

When you hit a word you don't know, drop into English for that word, then carry straight on in French. Je pense que c'est un NIGHTMARE pour les chevaux. It sounds messy on paper but in real conversation it keeps you moving. The French word will come later. Flow beats perfection.

Before Next Lesson

Practise dropping the three frames into everyday thoughts. Walking the dogs: il y a beaucoup de boue (there's lots of mud). Watching telly: je pense que c'est nul (I think it's rubbish). The slot can be anything. The frame is what matters.
