Finding Your Voice

Accent fundamentals · Your personal script · Farm, horses & family
Where We Started

First proper lesson done! We started with a quick accent test, then spent the rest of the session building your first script: a little run of French about you, the farm, your horses and James, that we drilled until it started coming out on its own. That's the method: talk about what you actually care about, repeat it until it's automatic, and let the grammar look after itself.

Accent Fundamentals

Your Welsh vowels are already doing you favours here: that rounded mouth shape is closer to French than flat English is. The main habit to build: relax the R and keep sounds further back in the mouth.

Merci beaucoup
mair-see bo-koo
Thank you very much

Watch the "beaucoup": rush the vowels and it drifts toward two words you really don't want to say to a waiter. Keep it deep and round: bo​-koo, not bo-kew.

Key Phrases
Je suis passionnée par…
zhuh swee pass-yon-ay par
I'm passionate about…
Je vis à la ferme
zhuh vee ah la fairm
I live on a farm
Le monde équestre
luh mond ay-kestr
The equestrian world
Ils s'appellent…
eel sa-pel
They're called… (for a list of names)
Mince !
manss
Oops! / Drat! The polite option. There are ruder versions if you want them, just ask.
Vocabulary

Farm & Horses

Un cheval shuh-val A horse
Des chevaux day shuh-vo Horses (plural, irregular!)
Une ferme oon fairm A farm
Un fils un fees A son (the final S is pronounced, unusually for French)

Sounding Natural

Un souvenir un soo-vuh-neer A memory
Un oubli un oo-blee A forgotten thing: the opposite of a souvenir
J'ai oublié zhay oo-blee-ay I forgot

Greetings & Register

Bonjour bon-zhoor Hello: shops, strangers, anyone you don't know well
Salut sa-loo Hi: friends, casual
Coucou koo-koo Hiya: close friends, kids, very affectionate
A Little Etymology
Un compagnon
un kom-pan-yon
A companion

Com- (with) + pain (bread): a companion is literally someone you share bread with. A lot of French vocabulary is hiding in plain sight like this once you know where to look for the English cousin underneath the accent.

Also: if you ever see a bottle of "La Vieille Ferme" (the rooster label) in the supermarket, now you know exactly what it means. The Old Farm.

Your Script: Learn This Off By Heart

This is the patter we built together. Order doesn't matter, hitting every beat does. Say it out loud a few times before bed. Repetition and sleep do the rest.

Bonjour !
bon-zhoor
Hello!
Je m'appelle Dawn.
zhuh ma-pel Dawn
My name's Dawn.
Je vis à la ferme et je suis passionnée par le monde équestre.
zhuh vee ah la fairm ay zhuh swee pass-yon-ay par luh mond ay-kestr
I live on a farm and I'm passionate about the equestrian world.
J'ai quatre chevaux et ils s'appellent Raven, Timothy, Alfie et Lily.
zhay katr shuh-vo ay eel sa-pel Raven, Timothy, Alfie ay Lily
I have four horses and they're called Raven, Timothy, Alfie and Lily.
Mince, j'ai oublié ! J'ai aussi un fils qui s'appelle James !
manss, zhay oo-blee-ay ! zhay oh-see un fees kee sa-pel James
Oops, I forgot! I've also got a son called James!
Before Next Lesson

No pressure and no memorising with flashcards. Just say the script out loud a handful of times when you think of it. It'll feel clunky tonight and noticeably less clunky by Friday. Next time: stretching the script further (where you live, what you're aiming for in France) and starting to handle being interrupted mid-sentence, which is most of what real conversation actually is.
