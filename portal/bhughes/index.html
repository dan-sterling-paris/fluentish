Your Get-Out-of-Jail-Free Cards

These three matter more than anything else on this page. They let you speak around a word you don't know instead of freezing. That's called circumlocution: circum = around, locution = speaking. You could know almost no French and still keep a conversation going with these three.

Comment dire... (+ the English word)
ko-mon deer — "common deer"
How do you say...?
Je veux dire...
zhuh vuh deer — "je vuh deer"
I want to say... / I mean...
Répétez, s'il vous plaît.
ray-pay-tay, see voo play
Repeat, please.

Use them without apology. Comment dire “hot milk”? is a perfectly good French sentence.

Your First Hinge Verbs — Être & Avoir

There are 11 hinge verbs that hold spoken French together, and être (to be) and avoir (to have) come first by a long way. We're learning French by sound, not spelling, so each verb is just two sounds: the je form, and one sound that covers tu, il, elle and on. Two sounds give you five sentences. Across all 11 verbs, 22 sounds give you 55.

Être — to be

Je suiszhuh sweeI am
Tu estoo ayyou are
Il esteel ayhe is
Elle estel ayshe is
On eston-naywe are

Learn swee and ay. That's the whole verb.

Avoir — to have

J'aizhayI have
Tu astoo ayou have
Il aeel ahe has
Elle ael ashe has
On aon-nawe have

Learn zhay and a. That's the whole verb.

The Beer Mat — Four Modals

Dan used to draw this grid on the back of beer mats in the pub when people asked how he learned French. Memorise the four modals, then drop any verb in after them. No conjugation needed: the second verb never changes. That's 160 sentences from one small grid.

Je peux... / Tu peux...
zhuh puh / too puh
I can... / You can...
Je veux... / Tu veux...
zhuh vuh / too vuh
I want to... / You want to...
Je dois... / Tu dois...
zhuh dwah / too dwah
I must... / You must...
Je vais... / Tu vas...
zhuh vay / too va
I'm going to... / You're going to...

The only one that changes is vais → vas with tu. Everything else stays the same.

Ordering a Cappuccino — the French Way

Forget the GCSE version. This is how people actually order. Walk in, say bonjour, then:

Bonjour ! Je prends un cappuccino, s'il vous plaît.
bon-zhoor ! zhuh pron un ka-poo-chee-no, see voo play
Hello! I'll have a cappuccino, please.
Je vais prendre un cappuccino, s'il vous plaît.
zhuh vay prondr un ka-poo-chee-no, see voo play
I'm going to have a cappuccino, please.
Je peux prendre un cappuccino, s'il vous plaît ?
zhuh puh prondr un ka-poo-chee-no, see voo play
Can I have a cappuccino, please?
Big, broad, confident

Staff switch to English when you whisper or shrink yourself, not because of your accent. Say it out loud, say it like you mean it, and they won't blink. Think of a French person in London saying “Hiya mate, how's it going?” in a thick French accent. Nobody switches to French on them. Rehearse it in your head on the way in, then deliver it.

Tips & Notes
Sound, not spelling

A baby says “more juice” long before they know how to spell it. We're doing French the same way round. Il est is just ay. There's no t on the end, and you don't need to know whether there's a silent s or t. Spelling comes later, once you start noticing the words you already say.

Use on for “we”

You'll have learned nous at school. In spoken French almost everyone says on, and it's easier because it takes the same sound as il and elle: on est, on a, on doit. You already know it from on y va (let's go). Keep nous for writing.

Don't worry about tu vs vous

Stay with tu for now. It's simpler. As a Brit you'll be let off if you use it with a waiter. A French person might come across as a bit rude; you won't.

Open your mouth

English speakers tighten their mouths and clip their words. French is the opposite: relaxed and open, almost as if you're talking with something in your mouth. Let peux, veux and vais stay loose.

Marron or marrant?

Marron (ma-ron) = brown. Marrant (ma-ron) = funny. They sound almost identical. Context does the work: Dolly est marron (Dolly is brown), Dolly est marrante (Dolly is funny).

Meilleur(e): “may-yuh”

The tricky one. Think MAY-YUHH. Meilleur and meilleure sound the same, so mon meilleur ami (a male best friend) and ma meilleure amie (a female best friend) only differ in the mon/ma at the front.

Ma maman / ta maman

Ma = my, ta = your. Ma maman = my mum, ta maman = your mum.

Hinge Verbs: Two Sounds Each

You used two structures with Dolly: Dolly est... (is) and Dolly a... (has). Those are your first two hinge verbs, and learning them as sounds gives you all five people at once.

Je suis zhuh swee I am
Tu es / il est / elle est / on est ay You are / he is / she is / we are
J'ai zhay I have
Tu as / il a / elle a / on a a You have / he has / she has / we have
Tu dois / il doit / on doit dwah You must / he must / we must (same idea, same sound)

Ordering Like You Mean It
Bonjour ! Je prends un cappuccino, s'il vous plaît.
bon-zhoor ! zhuh pron un ka-poo-chee-no, see voo play
Hello! I'll have a cappuccino, please.
Je vais prendre un cappuccino, s'il vous plaît.
zhuh vay prondr un ka-poo-chee-no, see voo play
I'm going to have a cappuccino, please.
Why it works

It's built from your Beer Mat: je vais + prendre. Add s'il vous plaît on the end and you've got a properly French order. When you practised it, you sounded French.

The Drills We Used
Ten minutes, no English

One topic (Dolly), three survival phrases on screen, and permission to be bumpy. The aim wasn't vocabulary. It was getting those three phrases so deep in that you use them without thinking.

Rhythm cycling

Dan clicked through the Beer Mat and you repeated what was highlighted, faster each round. You're a singer, so treat it as rhythm. Then: read it → eyes closed, screen still up → no screen at all. Swapping the order around doesn't matter, as long as you know what each one is.

Don't translate

You said you weren't thinking about the English, just looking at the frame. That's exactly the goal. Je peux should simply mean “I can”. Working out “je = I, peux = can” every time is too slow for a conversation.
