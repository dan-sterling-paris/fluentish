Pronunciation Tips
Singing off the back teeth

Imagine your back teeth and widen everything out. It feels forced at first, but overdo it and then bring it back. This is the technique that takes you from "good" to fluent.

French Rs

Don't roll it too much, and don't do a hard English R. Just chew on it lightly. A light English R is better than a bad French one.

Point vs Pont

Sur le point de (on the point of) vs sur le pont (on the bridge). The nasal sounds matter! Think "pwan" not "pon". Like the duck noise: quang quang.

Le français vs La française

Le français = the French language (masculine). Une française = a French woman (feminine). Be careful which one you mean!

Silent final letters

Les muscardins - don't pronounce the final S. Same for most French plurals. The "les" already tells you it's plural.

On vs Nous

In casual conversation, use on instead of nous. Nous sounds too formal for everyday French. On a vécu (we lived) not nous avons vécu.

Pendant, not Pour

For duration, use pendant (during/for), not pour. Saying "pour un an" is a classic English-speaker giveaway.

La France (feminine!)

La France, never "le France". The French won't care if you get Canada or Portugal wrong, but they will notice this one. Your protective armour.

Character & delivery

Get into character when you speak. Relax your shoulders, throw the phrase out like it's nothing. Your delivery transforms when you embody the intent rather than recite the words.

Ménage vs Déménager

Ménage = cleaning. Déménager = to move house. Similar etymology, completely different meanings.

Pronunciation Notes from This Lesson
"Le point" nasal sound

Think of how French ducks go "quang quang" - it's that nasal. If you say "pont" instead of "point", you're saying bridge instead of point. Overdo the nasality, then bring it back.

"Muscardin" endings

Words ending in -in have a nasal sound: muscardin, like vin (wine). Really lean into it. And don't pronounce the final S in the plural.

"Doucement" not "douche"!

Doucement = gently/slowly. Douche = a shower. Small but important difference! The "-ment" ending is soft: "dooss-mon".

Grammar & Pronunciation Notes
On vs Nous

When talking about your family or in casual conversation, use on instead of nous. Nous is technically correct but sounds too formal for everyday French, a bit like saying "one does" in English. On a vécu (we lived) not nous avons vécu.

Pendant, not Pour

For duration, use pendant (during/for), not pour. Saying "pour un an" is a classic English-speaker giveaway. On a vécu pendant un an à Paris or simply on a vécu un an à Paris.

La France (feminine!)

France is feminine: la France, never "le France". The French are fiercely proud of this. They won't care if you get Canada or Portugal wrong, but if you say "le France" they will notice. This is your protective armour.

Character & Delivery

Get into character when you speak. Like an actor changing emphasis on "To be or not to be", your delivery changes when you embody the intent. Shake your head, relax your shoulders, throw the phrase out like it's nothing. It transforms your pronunciation.

Ménage vs Déménager

Ménage = cleaning (faire le ménage). Déménager = to move house. The etymology looks similar but the meanings are completely different. Amener = to bring, so dé-ménager is literally to un-bring your home.
