Pronunciation Tips
Singing off the back teeth

Imagine your back teeth and widen everything out. It feels forced at first, but overdo it and then bring it back. This is the technique that takes you from "good" to fluent.

French Rs

Don't roll it too much, and don't do a hard English R. Just chew on it lightly. A light English R is better than a bad French one.

Point vs Pont

Sur le point de (on the point of) vs sur le pont (on the bridge). The nasal sounds matter! Think "pwan" not "pon". Like the duck noise: quang quang.

Le français vs La française

Le français = the French language (masculine). Une française = a French woman (feminine). Be careful which one you mean!

Silent final letters

Les muscardins - don't pronounce the final S. Same for most French plurals. The "les" already tells you it's plural.

Pronunciation Notes from This Lesson
"Le point" nasal sound

Think of how French ducks go "quang quang" - it's that nasal. If you say "pont" instead of "point", you're saying bridge instead of point. Overdo the nasality, then bring it back.

"Muscardin" endings

Words ending in -in have a nasal sound: muscardin, like vin (wine). Really lean into it. And don't pronounce the final S in the plural.

"Doucement" not "douche"!

Doucement = gently/slowly. Douche = a shower. Small but important difference! The "-ment" ending is soft: "dooss-mon".
